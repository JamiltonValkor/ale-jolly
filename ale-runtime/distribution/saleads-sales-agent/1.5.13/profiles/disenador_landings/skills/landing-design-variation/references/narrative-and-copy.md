# One idea from opening to close

Adapted from `references/conceptos.md`, `references/voz.md` and the source worksheet. Its twelve SaleADS stories contain useful narrative structures; their product capabilities and example numbers are not evidence for another business.

Start with a one-sentence thesis: **this visitor faces this substantiated tension; this offer addresses it through this supported mechanism; this evidence makes it credible; this configured action is the next step**. Keep it aligned with Ale's canonical bet. Structure follows the idea, not a mandatory hero/features/pricing/FAQ template.

## Twelve routes, translated into business decisions

| Source route | Reusable narrative and chapter movement | Conditions and exclusions |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `la-frase` | **A small first action:** show a clear entry action, what it starts, what happens next, what the visitor needs to decide, then return to the same action. | Use the actual WhatsApp/agenda/link flow. Do not invent a text composer, instantaneous result or automated fulfillment. |
| `el-equipo` | **Coordinated craft:** show the real product/service, the supported parts that work together, their roles, and what the customer receives. | Describe verified kit components or documented team/process only. No imported 24-agent roster, fictional employees or implied certifications. |
| `la-bitacora` | **Documented process:** a meaningful sequence with what happens and why, followed by the customer's checkpoint and the close. | Dates and logs require real sources. An explanatory sequence must not masquerade as live activity or a customer success record. |
| `el-boton` | **Choice and control:** frame a real decision, clarify the available options, show their conditions and guide a next action. | Only supported choices and genuine approval/return terms. No fabricated automation tiers, 48-hour undo, guarantee or interactive control. |
| `tu-plata` | **Transparent cost:** establish the exact offer/price, what is included, important conditions and what the action commits the visitor to. | Use one approved price source throughout. No invented savings, crossed-out anchor prices, discounts or agency-cost comparison. Missing terms go to the design worksheet, not a decorative certainty. |
| `del-mensaje-a-la-venta` | **A customer's path:** explain the documented path from interest to inquiry, purchase or service delivery using real product/process evidence. | Do not claim a sale occurred, invent a person or imply attribution without evidence. Show expected steps as steps, not measured outcomes. |
| `antes-y-despues` | **A relevant contrast:** make a substantiated difference in choice, workflow or product features easy to understand, then explain what stays constant. | No unsupported physical before/after, competitor caricature or causal performance claim. A side-by-side arrangement alone does not substantiate either side. |
| `un-dia-con-ale` | **A day or use occasion:** organize verified usage/process around a recognizable moment, with the product's role and the visitor's next decision. | Timing, frequency and results must be supported. Do not invent schedules, continuous monitoring or promise that the product works while someone sleeps. |
| `cada-numero` | **A number with meaning:** present a verified quantity, explain what it means, show its source/conditions, then connect it to the decision. | Price, pack quantity and verified specifications can be useful. Do not invent averages, success rates, review scores or conversion metrics. A platform click is not a sale. |
| `la-pieza` | **From input to tangible artifact:** foreground the real product or deliverable, reveal its supported components/details, show how it is used and close with the concrete offer. | Existing approved photos only. No generated usage scene, invented package contents, certification or claim that a platform has approved the offer. |
| `calma` | **Reassurance through clarity:** state a genuine objection, explain the documented process/limits and clarify the actual help or next step. | Calm tone comes from concrete information, not invented guarantees, support availability or a claim that problems cannot happen. |
| `aprende-mirando` | **Learn by observing:** explain a documented process or choice through a useful demonstration, then offer the actual next step. | Only when education helps this bet. No invented course, credential, automatic teacher or unimplemented product demo. |

These are starting structures, not twelve prescribed customer funnels. Merge or omit chapters when the real content warrants it. The opening and close should resolve the same thesis; a useful objection or material condition belongs where the visitor needs it, not buried in a final FAQ.

## Write eight headline candidates before selecting

1. Extract one approved fact/mechanism and the canonical tension. Note the evidence ID or brief field supporting it.
2. Draft eight genuinely different phrasings in the **page locale**. Explore a direct benefit within the approved promise, a concrete mechanism, a useful question, a real choice, product specificity or transparent cost as appropriate. Do not invent eight new claims.
3. Reject candidates that fit any unrelated brand after swapping its name, promise an unsupported outcome, imply a testimonial or require a nonexistent feature.
4. Read the viable ones as spoken language. Favor the one a visitor can understand at a glance and that survives actual mobile line breaks. A short headline is a tool, not permission to omit a material qualifier.
5. Record all eight and the selection reason in `design-direction.md`; use only the selected one in the page. A feedback revision can retain an already approved headline. Do not rewrite approved copy merely to repeat the exercise.

The source targets roughly 3–9 words (12 as a ceiling) and a short one- or two-sentence subhead. Use that as a concision check, adjusted for the actual locale and information needed. Do not fill missing proof with generic superlatives or manufacture urgency.

## Section grammar and voice

A useful section often has an optional meaningful label, a headline saying what changes for the visitor, one explanatory sentence, an actual product/evidence piece, and an action only when needed. Vary composition and rhythm instead of repeating this skeleton as identical cards.

Use the customer's documented voice and the trusted SaleADS locale. The source's third-person Ale, feminine first-person chat bubbles, product vocabulary, tuteo rules and fixed currency punctuation belong to its own brand/locale. They do not override another business or an English task. Format prices, dates and quantities for the approved locale/currency without changing their value.

Keep the transferable habits: the useful point first; concrete nouns and verbs; numbers with meaning and source; clear action labels; material price/terms close to the decision; concise prose; proportionate confidence. Essential conditions are readable body content, not faint decoration. A demonstration is labeled as such and does not imitate a real customer statement or observed outcome.

## Task worksheet and same-business comparison

Keep these in the existing task workspace, not in a global cross-business history:

- Business/task/landing/base-version references from the existing bindings.
- Narrative thesis; selected route and why it fits this bet.
- Headline candidates and chosen wording in the owner locale.
- Chapter sequence: idea, exact supporting asset/evidence and visitor question resolved.
- Thirteen-axis vector, real-photo map and one signature moment.
- Comparison to the exact previous revision and any earlier same-business direction supplied by Ale; preserve unknown history as unknown.
- Missing facts, concrete tradeoffs and review results.

On revision, retain this record and append the targeted changes/reasons. When a new design starts, Ale can provide these artifacts as same-business design context. This is design provenance; it does not decide what the curatorial learning system should admit or share.
