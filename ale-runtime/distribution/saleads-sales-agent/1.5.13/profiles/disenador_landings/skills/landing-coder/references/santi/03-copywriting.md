# Step 3 · Copywriting

English adaptation of **Santiago Tapias**, SaleADS source
`skills/v2/santi/03-textos.md`. Preserve the method and apply the supplied
business locale. Examples in this reference are synthetic illustrations,
never evidence or permission to make claims for the current business.

The source prioritizes clear copy over cosmetic changes. Treat that as a
working heuristic, not proof that one change caused sales. Write for a
hurried, skeptical visitor scanning a mobile screen.

## Voice

- **The customer's real words outrank clever wording.** Use phrases actually
  supplied by the owner or verified research, such as a genuine lack-of-time
  objection. Never turn an inferred pain or an AI draft into a verbatim quote.
- **Sound like this business:** warm when the actual voice is warm, restrained
  for a serious clinic. Reuse available posts/messages/context to establish tone.
  If essential voice evidence is missing, flag it to Ale instead of starting
  an unrequested interview or pretending to have read examples.
- **Register follows locale and supplied voice.** For Spanish, the source uses
  neutral LATAM `tú` by default; preserve real `vos`/`usted` usage when provided,
  without mixing them. Other locales need their natural equivalent, not Spanish
  imposed globally. Customer quotations can retain their original language as data.
- **Visitor before company.** Explain what the visitor gains more often than
  praising how good the business supposedly is.

## Main headline (H1)

- **Clarity before cleverness:** it must make sense on its own: what result,
  for whom. Preserve the canonical promise; the coder shapes its expression,
  not its substance.
- Add a time frame, quantity or condition only when explicitly supported.
  A statement about a first group of clients within a period is not a generic
  formula to copy into an unproven offer.
- **One main idea.** Place a second idea in the subheadline.
- Use exact figures for genuine results/proof when available; precision does
  not make an invented number acceptable.
- The **subheadline** explains credibility: actual mechanism, duration, effort
  or evidence, usually within about twenty words.
- Draft **three headline options** from different openings and choose according
  to awareness, without inventing three different strategic promises:
  1. Desired outcome for the real niche.
  2. Recognizable problem in the customer's real language.
  3. Actual mechanism, novelty or distinguishing method.
- If the headline promises a revelation or explains “the mistake,” the body
  must actually deliver that explanation.

## Section headings

Visitors often jump between headings; the first words must carry meaning.
Each heading should communicate without relying on the preceding paragraph.

- Weak generic labels: “Benefits,” “About us,” “Our services.”
- More informative directions: what the visitor can do, who will serve them,
  or which services/prices the section actually explains.

A heading is not improved merely by being longer; it needs a concrete purpose.

## Body

- **Short sentences:** one idea each; usually one to three mobile lines per
  paragraph. Shorten rather than compressing to a tiny font.
- **Accessible reading:** explain unfamiliar terms with examples. Keep domain
  words that the actual customer already uses; remove unnecessary jargon.
- **Benefit plus reason:** connect the gain to a real feature or process. Do
  not promise a response speed merely because an assistant could theoretically
  be fast.
- **Pain with a way forward:** a problem needs a useful method, plan or actual
  supported risk answer in the same/next screen. Do not agitate when there is
  no relevant solution and never attack or shame the person.
- **Give each genuine objection a place:** answer it in a specific block and,
  when useful, a FAQ phrased as the customer asks it. Risk doubts belong near CTA.
- **Real proof with context:** genuine situation → actual action → supported
  outcome, correctly attributed and authorized. Never write an AI testimonial.

## Action labels and microcopy

- Use a **specific verb**, usually at most three/four words, that fits one line:
  an actual booking, enquiry or other configured action. Avoid vague “Submit”
  and “Click here.” Translate naturally for the business locale.
- **One label per action** across the page; do not make identical destinations
  sound like different processes.
- Place a short, real reassurance below the button, such as an approved response
  expectation or known no-card requirement. Payment security, a seven-day
  guarantee or a one-hour response are examples requiring evidence, not defaults.
- Clear form-error copy is part of the source's broader method; it explains
  exactly how to correct a field. This pilot creates no form or form handler.

## Urgency

Only genuine urgency: state date, time, time zone, what changes afterward and
why. Explain a real enrollment close because a real group starts at a known
time. Avoid shouting capitals and empty “LAST CHANCE.” No resetting deadlines,
unverified capacity or urgency manufactured to fill a section.

## AI-sounding filler to remove

Replace with a supported detail or delete it:

- Generic superiority: comprehensive solutions, industry leaders, guaranteed
  quality, best in the market, unique, or price-on-request filler.
- Empty uplift verbs: empower, boost, elevate, take your business to the next
  level, revolutionize, transform your life, unlock your potential.
- Canned openings: dive in, discover the power of, in today's world, in the
  digital age, now more than ever.
- Formulaic contrast: “not just X, but Y,” “more than a course, an experience.”
- Adjective triplets, repeated rhetorical questions and repeated “Imagine…”
  openings that do not convey a real reason to act.
- Vague ease/efficiency: efficiently, simply, hassle-free, in just a few clicks.
- Em/en dash parenthetical asides in visitor-facing copy: prefer a period,
  comma or colon when that is natural for the locale.
- Unsupported timed income claims, guaranteed results, effortless/easy-money
  promises. Preserve the platform/business's approved claim restrictions.

## Locale-specific writing quality

For Spanish copy, retain sentence case in titles/buttons, explicit currency and
time zone when serving multiple countries, consistent local number formatting,
and opening question/exclamation marks. For another locale, use its grammar
and formatting while preserving the same factual content. Do not force Spanish
punctuation/register into every language.

## Final anti-filler pass

Before visual construction:

1. Read the headline aloud: could this owner naturally say it to a customer?
2. Remove the filler patterns above.
3. Replace empty adjectives with supported specifics; never invent a “48-hour”
   turnaround just to replace the word fast.
4. Check every number, name and testimonial against the supplied brief/evidence.
   Unsupported material is omitted and reported as missing to Ale. A production
   page should not expose `[TO CONFIRM]` or similar placeholders; an essential
   missing fact makes that part incomplete rather than licensed to invent it.
5. Shorten sentences longer than roughly twenty-five words where possible.
6. Remove consecutive paragraphs with identical openings/structure.
7. For Spanish, check that `tú`, `vos` and `usted` are not mixed accidentally;
   apply the equivalent voice consistency check to other locales.
8. Read only the headings from top to bottom: they should tell the page's
   complete argument.
